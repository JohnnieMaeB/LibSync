Groq's 429 handed me the arithmetic that had been missing:

> Limit 8000, Used 7966, Requested 3832.

On a $0 budget, tokens are the currency, and my system prompt was spending most of them on documents written for someone else.

## The ceiling

On the free tier, `gpt-oss-120b` allows 8,000 tokens per minute and 200,000 per day. At about 3,800 tokens a request, that's two requests a minute and roughly fifty a day. For a public assistant, that's not a limit. It's a ceiling.

## Measuring the prompt

I told Claude Code to figure out how to optimize the prompt and evaluate a replacement model. It measured the prompt component by component, estimating tokens as characters divided by four:

| Component | Estimated tokens |
|---|---|
| Persona and tool instructions | about 1,300 |
| RUSA reference guidelines | about 1,190 |
| ALA Library Bill of Rights | about 400 |
| ALA Core Values | about 710 |

The three documents I'd pasted in verbatim (Part 2) were **about 64% of the prompt.** To a model they read as things like "is easily identifiable as a staff member," "shares the search screen," "requests help from colleagues": behavior for a person at a desk. None of it is actionable by a chatbot, and all of it was paid for on every single request.

My mistake, made to look tidy by another agent's XML.

## The cut

Claude Code replaced the three documents with seven principles phrased as instructions:

1. Clarify when a question is ambiguous.
2. Make no assumptions and pass no judgment.
3. Help with any lawful topic, and give multiple perspectives on contested ones.
4. Ask for the minimum personal data.
5. Teach people how to evaluate sources.
6. Offer accessible formats.
7. When something is out of scope, say so and refer the patron to staff.

The sources are still cited in the code. The prompt shrank about 55%, and each request went from roughly 3,800 tokens to 2,200. On a per-model daily quota, that's about 40% more turns a day.

It also cut persona lines that invited invention. "Aware of all library workshops" became "only the ones the policy search returns."

I'm not certain the distilled principles preserve what a good reference librarian does. They preserve the behaviors a chatbot can act on, and I've checked that with evals, but I haven't had a librarian read them, and I'd like to.

## Then the builder slipped again

Claude Code's first CI eval run hit the 30-minute limit and printed nothing. The most likely cause was a daily-quota error it had never seen, hidden by buffered output, because its own local runs had already spent the day's tokens that production shares.

The fixes:

- Unbuffered, per-case logging so a run can't go silent.
- Parsing Groq's wait hints, in minutes and in hours.
- Failing fast when the wait is too long to be a per-minute limit.
- A three-minute cap per case.
- A schedule at a low-traffic hour.

Because Groq meters each model separately, the fallback chain is now three Groq models with separate daily buckets before the paid Hugging Face credit: `gpt-oss-120b`, then `qwen3.8-27b` (19 of 22 on the evals), then `gpt-oss-20b` (18 of 22).

## What I haven't measured

The new prompt scored 16 of 22 on the main model in the first scheduled run, and 20 of 22 after I fixed my own checks and the index (Part 14). I haven't measured how much of the improvement comes from the shorter prompt and how much from everything else I changed that week. I believe the cut didn't hurt, since the scores didn't fall, but I don't have a controlled comparison.

> **Architect's note:** delegate goals, not steps ("optimize the prompt, evaluate the model"), and demand the numbers. The 64% is why I trust the cut.
