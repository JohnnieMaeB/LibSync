3,832 tokens

February 9, 2027

Groq's 429 handed me the arithmetic that had been missing: "Limit 8000, Used 7966, Requested 3832." On a $0 budget, tokens are the currency, and my system prompt was spending most of them on documents written for someone else.

On the free tier, gpt-oss-120b allows 8,000 tokens per minute and 200,000 per day. At about 3,800 tokens a request, that's two requests a minute and roughly fifty a day. For a public assistant, that's not a limit. It's a ceiling.

I told Claude Code to figure out how to optimize the prompt and evaluate a replacement model. It measured the prompt component by component: persona and tool instructions about 1,300 tokens, RUSA guidelines about 1,190, ALA Library Bill of Rights about 400, ALA Core Values about 710.

The three documents I pasted in verbatim (Part 2) were 64% of the prompt. To a model they read like "is easily identifiable as a staff member," "shares the search screen," "requests help from colleagues": behavior for a person at a desk, none of it actionable by a chatbot, all of it paid for on every request. My mistake, made to look tidy by another agent's XML.

It replaced them with seven principles phrased as instructions (clarify when ambiguous; no assumptions or judgment; any lawful topic, multiple perspectives on contested ones; minimum personal data; teach evaluation; offer accessible formats; when out of scope, say so and refer to staff), sources still cited in code. The prompt shrank about 55%, each request from roughly 3,800 to 2,200 tokens. It also cut persona lines that invited invention: "aware of all library workshops" became "only the ones the policy search returns."

Then the builder slipped again. Its first CI eval run hit the 30-minute limit and printed nothing (buffered output and, most likely, a daily-quota 429 it had never seen), because its own local runs had already spent the day's tokens that production shares. The fixes: unbuffered per-case logging, parsing Groq's minute and hour wait hints, fail-fast on waits too long to be per-minute, a 3-minute cap per case, a low-traffic schedule. And since Groq meters per model, the chain is now three Groq models with separate daily buckets before the Hugging Face credit: gpt-oss-120b, then qwen3.8-27b (19 of 22), then gpt-oss-20b (18 of 22).

Architect's note: delegate goals, not steps ("optimize the prompt, evaluate the model"), and demand the numbers. The 64% is why I trust the cut.
